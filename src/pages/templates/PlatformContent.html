How it is built

Not a bundle of tools. A single data layer.

Most private companies manage ownership across disconnected systems: spreadsheets for cap tables, law firms for documents, email threads for investor communication.

Arcstone is built around one shared ownership record. Equity management, investor onboarding, governance, document workflows, reporting, and lifecycle administration all operate from the same underlying data layer. No duplicated records. No unnecessary migration. No mismatch between your cap table and your other records.

You extend the ownership system you already use as your company grows.

Platform modules

Three modules. One record.

Module 01

Equity management

Arcstone maintains a structured, real-time cap table that tracks every shareholder, share class, round, transfer, and ownership event. This is the foundation — the single source of truth every other module builds on.

  • Cap table with full round and transfer history
  • SAFEs, notes, and warrants tracked as structured, calculable instruments with conversion terms computed automatically
  • Vesting schedules, valuations, and share-based compensation expense calculated and versioned
  • A live readiness score that flags what needs fixing before a raise, an audit, or a new holding
  • Every investor right and company obligation tracked against its own document, deadline, and status
  • Bring an existing cap table in from a spreadsheet or another platform, with conflicts flagged before anything is applied
  • Complete audit trail for every ownership event, correctable only through a linked reversal

Module 02

Investor workflows

Investor onboarding, document collection, eligibility checks, participation records, and investor communications — managed inside the same platform as the cap table, not across separate tools and email threads.

  • Structured onboarding with identity and eligibility checks
  • Every investor tracked from first contact through diligence, commitment, signature, and funding in a single pipeline
  • AI-assisted discovery that matches the company against relevant funds, accelerators, and grants, ranked by fit
  • Digital agreements connected to investor records
  • One identity record per investor shared between the fundraising pipeline and the ownership register
  • Investor updates and reporting from within the platform

Module 03

Lifecycle administration

Post-raise is where ownership operations usually break down: manual distributions, scattered investor records, and transfer requests handled through email. Arcstone brings the full lifecycle, governance, and reporting into one administration environment.

Governance

Board resolutions, shareholder resolutions, and class consents move through the platform with configurable approval thresholds — simple majority, supermajority, or unanimous — and their own notice periods, from draft through counsel review to signature and adoption. A compliance calendar tracks statutory deadlines for the company's jurisdiction alongside deadlines generated automatically from its own cap table, instruments, and recorded rights.

Reporting and audit

Every material action is written to an append-only audit trail that cannot be edited or deleted. Board-ready reports and accountant-ready exports are generated directly from the ledger and clearly labelled as informational inputs — not tax, accounting, or legal advice — so professionals can build on them without ambiguity.

  • Distribution calculations and entitlement tracking
  • Transfer restrictions and eligibility-controlled movement
  • Corporate events: new rounds, buybacks, restructures
  • Exit administration and proceeds entitlement, modelled in advance through scenario planning

Architecture

One data layer, not several tools.

Most ownership platforms are assembled from components built separately. Data moves between them through imports, exports, and manual reconciliation. Arcstone was built as a single layer from the start.

Every action in any module — a new shareholder, a signed agreement, a filed resolution, a distribution — writes to and reads from the same underlying record. The cap table, the compliance calendar, and the investor database are all the same thing.
